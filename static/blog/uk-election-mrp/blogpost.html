Predicting Labour's 2024 Performance with Bayesian MRP

Bayesian inference R / brms Python / bambi UK politics   Work in progress

Multilevel regression with post-stratification (MRP) is the technique behind YouGov's seat-level polling — used to call the 2019 and 2024 elections more accurately than conventional polling. This project applies MRP to the British Election Study (BES) panel to estimate Labour vote share at constituency level across all 575 England & Wales constituencies, and validates those estimates against the actual 2024 General Election results.

What is MRP?

The core idea: fit a multilevel model to a national survey that learns how vote intention varies by demographics (age, education, past vote, etc.). Then use census data to describe the demographic composition of each constituency. Combine the two to produce a constituency-level prediction — essentially re-weighting the survey to match each local area.

Standard constituency polls cost £10,000–£20,000 each and would take millions to cover all 650 seats. MRP does it from a single national survey of ~10,000–30,000 respondents. The multilevel structure is crucial: it borrows strength across constituencies, so that even seats with few survey respondents get sensible estimates.

Models

Two implementations for comparison — both Bayesian, both producing posterior distributions over constituency vote shares:

  • brms (R) — Bayesian multilevel models via Stan; random effects for constituency, region, age group, and education
  • bambi (Python) — equivalent model in Python using the bambi formula interface, also backed by Stan/PyMC

Key results

  • RMSE ~8 percentage points across all 575 constituencies — in line with published academic MRP benchmarks at this level of model complexity
  • brms and bambi produce near-identical posterior means; differences lie in sampling efficiency
  • The model captures the broad geography of the Labour landslide but undershoots in strongly Conservative-to-Labour swing seats
